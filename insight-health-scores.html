Customer Analytics

Health scores are signals, not answers.

A score can help prioritize attention. It cannot replace judgment or a real customer conversation.

Health scores are useful when they simplify meaningful customer signals. They become dangerous when teams treat the number as objective truth.

Start with the decision

Before building a score, define what action it should support. Risk outreach, adoption coaching, executive alignment, expansion, and renewal forecasting may require different signals.

Use explainable inputs

A Customer Success Manager should understand why an account is green, yellow, or red. Product usage, support activity, stakeholder engagement, outcomes, sentiment, and commercial timing all matter, but each input should have a clear reason for existing.

Keep room for context

A healthy usage pattern can hide a budget problem. Low usage can be normal for a seasonal customer. A new executive sponsor can change an account faster than any historical trend.

The practical takeaway

Use health scores to focus attention, then validate the signal with account knowledge and direct conversation.
