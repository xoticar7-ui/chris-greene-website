Customer Success

Customer Success should not begin at renewal.

Retention is the outcome of everything that happened before the renewal conversation.

A renewal is not a standalone event. It is the customer’s verdict on onboarding, adoption, support, communication, business value, and trust.

The renewal problem usually starts earlier

When a team waits until the final 90 days to address risk, it is often reacting to problems that began during handoff, implementation, or the first missed outcome. The commercial deadline simply makes those problems impossible to ignore.

Build retention into the lifecycle

Strong Customer Success teams define ownership early, establish success criteria, create meaningful milestones, and maintain a clear view of adoption, value, risk, and executive alignment. Renewal then becomes a continuation of demonstrated value, not a rescue mission.

Use data, but keep the conversation

Health scores and product usage can identify signals. They cannot explain every change in customer priorities, leadership, budget, or internal politics. The operating model must combine data with direct customer context.

The practical takeaway

Do not build a renewal process in isolation. Build a customer lifecycle that makes renewal the natural next decision.
